# Radio horizon

The Horizon tab shows how far a site can see.

## Using it

1. Choose the **site**, its **antenna height** and the height of the **receivers** (1.5 m for people and cars; 0 m shows the bare ground).
2. Set how far out to look (by default the site's terrain distance).
3. Press **Show horizon**.

It updates as you change the heights, the distance or the site.

## What it shows

- **Yellow** on the map: ground where a receiver at that height has a clear line to the antenna, over the curved earth (k = 4/3). Grey is hidden ground.
- **Orange line**: how far that reaches on each of 1,440 bearings.
- **Panorama** under the map: the skyline all the way round as seen from the antenna. It plots the elevation angle against bearing (N, E, S, W), with level and the **smooth-earth horizon** marked. Hover for the bearing, the skyline angle and distance, and how far receivers can see the site that way.
- **Summary**: the smooth-earth horizon distance, the area that can see the antenna, and the average and furthest reach.

Line of sight is not the same as coverage: VHF and UHF signals also bend over hills, which the Coverage tab models. The horizon view is for seeing what blocks a site and where a higher mast would help.
